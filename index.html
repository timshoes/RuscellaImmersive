What your team leaves with

The 100 Immersive Labs Initiative is building a national network of campus labs where educators and students create immersive learning, not just consume it. The summer cohort is where each lab begins.

  • An operating lab

    Workstations set up and in use, with a repeatable workflow from first idea to headset.

  • A trained team

    Three to twelve faculty and students who can take a project through planning, capture, editing and deployment.

  • Experiences ready to use

    Finished 360 VR work built around the subject your institution chose, ready for students in the fall.

  • A roadmap and operations manual

    A clear path to activate the lab across campus and grow it into a lasting program.

Six weeks, from idea to headset

Live sessions on Eastern time, with team work between sessions. Schools in every time zone are welcome. Exact dates are shared at the info sessions.

  1. Week 1Plan

    Choose the story, the learners and the outcome. Build the shot list.

  2. Week 2Capture

    360 camera fundamentals, in the field and on location.

  3. Week 3Convert

    Stitch, prepare and organize footage for editing.

  4. Week 4Edit

    Shape the narrative with sound, narration and points of interest.

  5. Week 5Deploy

    Publish to headsets and test with real learners.

  6. Week 6Showcase

    Present the work and map what your lab does next.

Built for colleges ready to start

Designed for community colleges and four-year institutions alike, from liberal arts colleges to HBCUs, whether you have never used XR or already own a few headsets and want to bring creation into the arts and humanities.

Your team

  • A champion who will lead your XR work beyond the summer
  • 3 to 12 participants: faculty, students or both
  • A subject or project you want to bring to life

Your setup

  • One workstation for every three participants: a development computer, a 360 camera and a VR headset
  • A room to work in, such as an existing lab or classroom

Not set up yet? We will make sure your team has the equipment, software and space it needs before the cohort begins, and help you buy only what you need.

Ways to fund your seat

Colleges commonly fund immersive learning through the programs below. Eligibility and deadlines vary, and we can help you prepare your application.

  • Perkins V

    Federal career and technical education funds for programs that prepare students for skilled work.

  • NSF Advanced Technological Education

    National Science Foundation grants focused on technician education at two-year colleges.

  • Title III and Title V

    Strengthening-institutions grants for HBCUs, Hispanic-serving and other minority-serving institutions.

  • State workforce funds

    State and regional workforce development grants for in-demand skills.

  • Federal Work-Study

    Pays student production team members while they build real experience.

  • Innovation and donor funds

    Institutional innovation budgets, foundations and donors, as at CNU.

Questions

Is the cohort in person?

No. It is live and virtual, on Eastern time, so teams anywhere in the country can take part.

Do we need prior XR experience?

No. Teams range from complete beginners to computer science programs expanding into the arts and humanities. Your onboarding plan starts where you are.

What if we don’t have equipment yet?

Tell us in your application. During onboarding we confirm what you have, recommend what you need, and help you get it in place before the cohort begins.

What happens after the summer?

You leave with a roadmap and operations manual to activate the lab yourselves. Institutions that want a partner for that stage can continue working with us.

Who should be our champion?

Someone with the standing and interest to lead XR on campus after the summer: often a faculty member, program director or department head.
