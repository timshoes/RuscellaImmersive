JJ Ruscella directs. Over thirty years that has meant stage plays, a feature film, a university donor gala with two hundred performers, live simulations for police and educators, and immersive experiences worn on the head rather than watched from a seat. The medium keeps changing. The job does not.

He trained as a theatre artist, earning his MFA at Rutgers University's Mason Gross School of the Arts, and spent twelve years at the University of Central Florida teaching theatre and film. There he founded the Interactive Performance Lab with UCF's School of Film and Digital Media, developing techniques for simulation actors and new forms of live-action simulation and immersive theatre. That work — putting a real person inside a staged situation and making it count — is the through line to everything since.

He went on to build the Shenandoah Center for Immersive Learning from concept into a student-run immersive design and production lab, earning the Virginia Foundation for Independent Colleges’ H. Hiter Harris III Award for Excellence in Instructional Technology, to co-author a peer-reviewed taxonomy for immersive experience design that now underpins how the U.S. Air Force teaches XR to commanders, and to create OrchestrateXR, software for designing and orchestrating immersive learning experiences. He co-founded Ruscella Immersive and directs its productions.
