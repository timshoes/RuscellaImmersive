The challenge

Funding without a plan is the most common place to get stuck.

Donor funding had unlocked the door, but a $150K first allocation to deploy in four months left no room for false starts. Faculty ranged from experienced digital producers to people who joked they couldn't turn on a computer, all in the same room, all needing to ship real work.

  1. No strategic frameworkNo clarity on what the lab was, who it served, or how it would be sustained.
  2. A compressed timelineEvery decision had to hold up on the first pass.
  3. Mixed technical readinessFrom expert producers to self-described tech avoiders.
  4. The fear of failing publiclyFaculty needed a culture of safety, not just a curriculum.

Two phases. One blueprint.

Phase 01

Foundation

Assessment · Roadmap · Lab design · Build

We embedded with CNU to understand its culture, people and vision before a line was drawn or a dollar spent. A former computer lab became a themed “time-ship”: a space that earns its place on the campus tour, not just the syllabus. All hardware was procured and configured with Apple and Insight Public Sector.

Phase 02

Activation

Workshops · Project development · Launch

Six faculty, eight weeks. We started with 360: familiar enough to feel possible, immersive enough to feel new. Every participant shipped a finished experience tied to their own discipline. No demos, no homework, real published work. CNU then joined the NEXTWave Network.

A creative lab, not a gadget room.

  • EnvironmentA themed “time-ship”

    Layout, workstation flow and thematic detail that invite curiosity from the moment you walk in.

  • Capture360 cameras · iPad & iPhone

    Field-ready capture for performances, historical sites and locations. Faculty took cameras out on day one.

  • Create & editApple Mac Studio

    Stitch, trim, annotate, layer in spatial audio and narration, with a toolchain built for faculty rather than specialists.

  • Deploy & experienceApple Vision Pro · Meta Quest

    Multiple stations where students are co-creators, not just consumers.

  • OperationsManaged device enrollment · AppleCare

    Low IT overhead, so the focus stays on teaching and learning.

A federal research partnership with NOAA.

Within three months the lab had attracted the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration as a research partner, turning a campus learning space into a collaborator for government-grade immersive work.

The work

  • HistoryPhil

    Took a 360 camera to Revolutionary War sites so students can stand where Henry Knox stood while he narrates what happened and why it still matters.

    Students describe feeling the weight of what Knox accomplished.
  • EnglishMary

    A literary tour of New Orleans as a 360 reading environment. She began with zero comfort with technology and finished as filmmaker, sound engineer and storyteller.

    Her students now walk the city with her and find their own stories.
  • MusicMax

    A playground for spatial audio: cameras and microphones in unusual spaces, mood as curriculum. The lab became his instrument.

    Students spend twice as long with the work as any other assignment format.
  • ClassicsDavid

    Years of 360 capture from Rome and Pompeii, reshaped into narrated field trips students stand inside.

    Students call the Pompeii experience the most memorable moment of their semester.
